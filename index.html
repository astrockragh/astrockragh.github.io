The short version

I like modelling and measuring.

I build statistical and machine-learning models to learn about galaxies through cosmic time, from few to many, from near to far. I always aim to have the models I build carry real physics inside them — inductive biases, geometry, and hard constraints — and to calibrate my posteriors over and over. As a modeller, I like working with the full set of the astronomical, from the instrument to the statistical inference to the theoretical models: from optimising spectrographs by understanding the atmosphere’s own emission, to geometric models of how galaxies grow inside the cosmic web.

Research directions

Four directions, centered around a common goal. Better data analysed with better models → better understanding of the Universe.

01

Galaxy environments and assembly with GNNs

Halo merger histories and large-scale environment encoded as graphs, and modelled with Graph Neural Networks.

See the projects →

02

Clustering and cosmic variance in the high-z Universe

Using the clustering of the earliest galaxies to constrain and explain early galaxy physics — or marginalizing it away when we do not know it.

See the projects →

03

Data-driven science with spectra

Spectra are the most information-rich data type we have, and they are the cornerstone of galaxy property inference. Whether for galaxy evolution or instrument calibration, we can do better science and make better models by using them more effectively.

See the projects →

04

Instrument optimization

Calibration from the Earth's sky itself. Airglow, sky subtraction and wavelength solutions for the Prime Focus Spectrograph, plus PSF work for COSMOS2020.

See the projects →
